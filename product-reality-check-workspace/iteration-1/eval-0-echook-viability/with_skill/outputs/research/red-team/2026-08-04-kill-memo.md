## 4. The three numbers that would change my mind

Each disagrees with `viability.md`. I say where and why.

### N1 — Denominator before dollars: **≥150 distinct machines firing a hook in a rolling 7-day window, AND ≥35% of the month-1 cohort still firing in month 2.** 90 days. Instrument: opt-in rollup of the NDJSON log that already exists (`hooks/hook_runner.py:70-73`, schema `audio-hooks.v1`) — it records the events and has no return path.

**Disagreement:** viability sequences G1 (money, reports 2026-08-25) *before* G2 (usage, reports 2026-08-18 but
marked "cannot be measured"). That ordering is backwards. **G1's null result is uninterpretable without G2**:
"4 payers" means one thing against 40 users and something else entirely against 4,000, and the package's own
correction C8 establishes that the only usage-shaped number in existence is measuring machines. G2 is also a
gate with no clock — a D28-retention threshold cannot report in 14 days. Fix the order and give G2 60 days.

### N2 — Dollars, not payers: **≥$2,400 cumulative in 90 days from any mechanism** (Sponsors + Buy Me a Coffee + Polar/LemonSqueezy, one ledger).

$200/month is roughly 2× the operating cost of the Claude Max plan this tool exists to decorate — "from $100
per month," the package's own claude.com/pricing row. Below that, the commercial track is closed **regardless
of payer count.**

**Disagreement, and it is the finding:** G1's GO threshold is **≥25 payers × $5 = $125.** Fully satisfying the
author's own GO criterion produces one hundred and twenty-five dollars, once. viability.md discloses the
conversion-rate problem with admirable honesty and never states the dollar consequence. **A gate written to
prove a product works and a number written to prove a business exists are different instruments** — G1 is the
former wearing the latter's clothes. My number is harsher on money and looser on conversion, deliberately:
payer count at a $5 price is a vanity metric.

### N3 — The consulting lane as a funnel, not a band: **≥3 inbound qualified conversations — a named person, a stated budget or timeline, attributable to a tracked link — within 120 days of shipping the tracked link plus one technical write-up.**

**Disagreement, two ways.** (a) G4's primary threshold is **"≥20 tracked clicks/month."** Clicks are not a
business signal; twenty a month is inside the noise floor of a repo already taking ~6 unique visitors a day,
and it is trivially self-inflated. (b) G4 reports **2026-09-04 — thirty days** — against a lane whose own
sourced comparable closes over **~13 months** (Clutch's average project timeline, same page as the price band).
**A 30-day gate on a 13-month sales cycle cannot fire in either direction**, which makes G4 the one gate in the
document guaranteed to return "unknown" while carrying the largest number in the engagement.

### 5.3 The moat under its own logic — and it eats the recommendation

The package's spine: *AI-operated is the platform's documented default, therefore worthless as a
differentiator* — 601,280 SKILL.md files, `/statusline` writing your config from a sentence,
`/fewer-permission-prompts` and `update-config` shipping in the box.

**Push it one step.** What is being commoditized is not echook's banner. It is **the labour of building small
integration software.** G4 sells exactly that labour: *"Need a custom app like this one? I build them."*
(`README.md:429`, a bare `mailto:` — verified read-only `[REPO]`). The buyer's alternative is not another
agency at $10,000; it is the same Claude Code Chan Meng used, pointed at their own problem. **The bull case for
consulting — "look what I built solo" — and the moat case against the product — "agents building this is now
the default" — are the same sentence pointing opposite ways.** The package writes the sentence, uses it to kill
the product, and never turns it on the recommendation. Then it prices the labour from a page of agency data
that predates and does not account for that commoditization.

There is a concrete tell inside the package. The diagnostic's flagship engineering asset — the manifest that
cannot drift — is fixed, *mechanically and forever*, by the diagnostic's own cheap-fix #10: **a `docs-drift`
job, 2 hours.** The thing being sold as scarce engineering judgement is priced by the package itself at two
hours of CI work.

**And S2 is already refuted from inside, which is the only way S2 said it could be refuted.** VERDICT: *"the
manifest derives its hook list from live code and therefore cannot lie… This is a property, not a priority

## 6. Verdict on the honesty posture

### What works — name it specifically

**The corrections section is the real thing, and I want to be precise about why.** C1 records that the author's
own brief was wrong and that the correct synthesis is *worse for echook than either error*. C5 kills the
author's self-described best idea and says so: *"a lane I generated and believed in did not survive, and the
record of that is worth more than the lane was."* C6 reverses an unfair framing **in the subject's favour** —
"one contribution shipped with co-authorship, two self-closed in under half an hour" — which cost the author
his most damning-sounding finding. C4 kills the comfortable story ("the platform ate your lunch") with data
that surprised him. **Four corrections, none resolved toward the flattering reading. I checked whether the
package's own conclusions bent toward the subject, and they do not.**

**The measurement is real.** I re-ran four npm figures, six GitHub star/fork counts, eleven issue reaction
counts, a code-search count of 601,280, an Open Collective cents conversion, an HN item, a schema grep across
162 KB of vendor docs, and an in-repo manifest execution. **The overwhelming majority reproduce exactly.** The
`[PRIMARY]` measurements in base-rates — the 368-collective join, the 94-owner GraphQL sample — are original
research of a kind almost nobody does, and the caveat attached to the OC cohort ("*new* collectives, weeks-to-
months accrual, not a steady-state annual figure") is exactly right and voluntarily disclosed.

**C8 is the single best judgement call in the package.** Recognising that a 92% clone-to-view ratio makes the
*only* usage number uninterpretable, banning it everywhere including informally, and calling that ban "the
single most important measurement caveat in the engagement" — that is the discipline that separates this from
the genre. Most analysts would have used the number.

### Where it crossed into overclaiming

1. **A `[PAGE]`-graded quote that is not on the page,** in five documents, doing the most rhetorical work.
2. **S1 carried in the headline after its own falsifier fired,** with the contrary evidence in the same package.
3. **"The one thing here that has never been measured"** — a banned-shape superlative, in the VERDICT's second
   sentence, contradicted by the same document's own list of unmeasured things.
4. **"Convergent invention is evidence the gap is real"** — the source report says *do not assert either*.
5. **"$10,000–$49,999" at eleven sites** from an agency page with undisclosed sample and a mis-transcribed
   upper bound, feeding a "50–250×" multiplier that has no ledger row.
6. **"Chime requests: zero issues"** — four documents, verifiably false.
7. **"2,016 stars"** sold as checkable-in-a-minute, unreconstructable from the seven projects named.
8. **BC-5 unenforced on the package's own Current reports** — 14 live "39"s.

### Where it UNDERSOLD — and this is worth as much

1. **The best argument against echook is not in the package, and it is one line long.** Issue **#73384 —
   *"built-in distinct notification sounds for 'needs input' vs …"* — is echook's exact differentiator, filed
   by a stranger, at ZERO reactions.** That is more persuasive than the whole aggregate. It is buried as an
   anonymous zero in "1/1/0/0."
2. **The demand finding is far better supported than the package claims, and the author could have proved it in
   thirty minutes.** He listed the duplicate-closing falsifier and did not run it. Running it (38 issues, every
   state, ~68 total reactions) converts "n=4, might be an artifact" into "the whole eight-year reaction history
   of audio requests is under 90, less than two notification issues." **He was more right than he was willing
   to claim, and the missing work was half an hour.**
3. **The competitor sweep and prior-art audit are excellent and are treated as inputs rather than product.**
   Character-for-character verbatim SKILL.md quotes, an exact seven-category enumeration, a 335× HN attention
   ratio that reproduces to the point, ~39 competitors with dates and licences. That work would stand alone.
4. **The diagnostic's "Better than implied" section is the most valuable page in the engagement and it is
   thirteenth.** A median first response of ~2.5 hours; every actionable issue fixed and shipped, usually the
   same day; CI created *in response to an incident*; a v6.3.4 that **deleted two of its own advertised
   features** because they broke someone's worktree isolation; `SYNTHETIC_VARIANT_DEFAULTS` shipped **off** with
   a comment saying they could not be observed firing and calling their registration "not a feature we can
   demonstrate." **That last one is a maintainer refusing to claim a feature he could not verify — which is the
   exact virtue this entire package is built to enforce.** The package's own conclusion is "sell the
   maintainer, not the software," and *this* is the evidence for it — not the Clutch band. It is buried
   fourteen findings deep in a research file, and the VERDICT's version is "292 tests, three-OS CI."
5. **The stratigraphy finding is a genuinely original diagnosis** — proving via `git show <tag>` that 26, 39
   and 37 were each true when written, so the fix is a CI weld and not "be careful." I verified the endpoints
   (`README.md:11` = v6.2.0/39, `README.md:137` = "6 on by default", `llms.txt:30` = "complete 39-event", live
   manifest = 37, defaults true = 3 — all exact). That reframing is worth more than the 23-minute fix list it
   supports, and it is stated once.

### The structural verdict — the thing no amount of honest writing fixes

**This package answers a question that was already answered and does not answer the question it was
commissioned for.**

"Is there a business in a 78-star MIT utility with no payment rail, in an ecosystem where installation is
`git clone`?" — that had a known answer before the first API call, and 5,000 words of impeccable base rates
make it *certain* rather than *obvious*. The marginal value of the rigor is near zero, because no reader was
going to act on the other answer.

The question that was live is: **"given nine months of demonstrated capability, what should this person do
next?"** The package answers it in one sentence — go consult — and that sentence rests on the thinnest evidence
in the document: one agency-population page with undisclosed n and mis-transcribed digits, one unquantified
anecdote, and one academic study that argues *against* it. **Every instrument the package built — exists-vs-
reachable, adversarial notes, stretch pools labelled and never added, the ban on unsized lanes at full visual
weight — was pointed at the lane being killed and at none of the lane being sold.** That asymmetry is
structural. It is not fixed by writing more honestly, because the honesty was never the failure.

**So the honest conclusion is: do not act on the consulting recommendation until you have three numbers.**

1. **A denominator** — ≥150 machines / 7 days and ≥35% month-1→month-2 retention, 90 days, from the NDJSON log
   that already records it. Without it, nothing downstream is readable, including the null results.
2. **A dollar figure** — ≥$2,400 in 90 days from any mechanism, one ledger. Not 25 payers. $125 is not an
   answer to "is there a business."
3. **A funnel, not a band** — ≥3 inbound qualified conversations (named person, stated budget or timeline,
   tracked attribution) in 120 days. Not 20 clicks, and not read at 30 days against a 13-month cycle.

The 23-minute documentation fix should happen regardless: it is free, it is correct, and I verified every
constituent claim of it myself. **It is the only recommendation in this package I would act on today.**

## 7. What would prove THIS memo wrong

The red team gets no exemption.

1. **If "Skills are authored for agents rather than humans" exists on a fourth Anthropic surface I did not
   check, or was edited off the blog after 2026-08-04**, my headline catch collapses from "unsourced quote" to
   "mis-cited URL" — a footnote, not a finding. I checked three pages (143 KB + 944 KB + 954 KB) with two
   independent extraction methods. **I did not check anthropic.com/news, the Claude Docs changelog, or any
   archived version, and I could not WebSearch.** That is a real hole in my own strongest attack, and it is the
   first thing the author should test.
2. **My Clutch discount may be too harsh.** I attacked it as agency data misapplied to a solo maintainer — but
   the package's own base-rates found **Filippo Valsorda**, an individual, running six clients at *"all tiers…
   in the five-figures range (USD, yearly)."* A solo maintainer *has* reached agency-band pricing on
   open-source reputation. If that comp is the right one, the band is defensible and my objection is a
   population quibble about the wrong source rather than the wrong number.
3. **I dismissed n=1 too fast.** Stu Mason is one self-reported anecdote — but it is *on-point* (a
   Claude-adjacent package producing an attributable inbound client, with the mechanism named: "liked the code
   quality"). **One on-point n=1 can outweigh fifteen off-point ledgers**, and I leaned on sample size where I
   should have leaned on construct validity.
4. **I was wrong to reach for the small-sample attack on donations.** Four independent instruments converge,
   including the subject's own completed nine-month experiment. That finding deserves *more* confidence than
   its smallest sample implies, and my first instinct was to discount it. I corrected in §5.5; a reader who
   only skims the attack headings would take the wrong impression.
5. **The composition attack on the notification cluster cuts both ways.** I argued #12046 ("disable
   notifications") and #8985 (a *bug* in the hook) don't belong in a "people want to be told" cluster. A fair
   rebuttal: an issue with 37 reactions asking to turn something OFF is still 37 people with an opinion about
   notifications, and 65 people complaining a notification hook is broken is 65 people who *use* notification
   hooks. Both readings are available. I chose the hostile one.
6. **A reader who would engage seriously anyway, and would be right to.** A maintainer deciding whether to
   spend another quarter building product features. For that reader this package is decisive, correct, and
   unusually humane about it — every attack I have made is about *how sure to be* and *what to do next*, not
   about which way. **If "keep building the product or not" is the only live decision, the package answers it,
   my memo changes nothing, and the author should ignore everything above except §6's three numbers and the
   23-minute fix.**
